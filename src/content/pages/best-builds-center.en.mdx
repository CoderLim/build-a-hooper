## Best Center Build

Centers still own the paint — rebounding, rim protection, finishing — even as many fives now stretch or pass ([Nike on modern centers](https://www.nike.com/a/positions-in-basketball)). Public archetype work keeps “traditional / anchor big” and “all-around high-usage big” as separate animals ([cluster examples](https://github.com/Cubin1215/NBA-Archetype-classification-and-performance)). In Build a Hooper, start from **REB + IDEF**, then choose finisher, protector, or unicorn creation.

## Position identity

If REB is empty, you are not a center — you are a tall mismatch waiting to lose the season on the glass. If IDEF and BLK are both empty, opponent drivers will live in the lane for 82 games.

## Priority order (Classic)

1. **REB**
2. **IDEF**
3. **BLK** or **FIN** (protector vs finisher fork)
4. **STR**
5. **DNK** / **ATH** / optional **3PT** or **PAS** for specialty routes

## Three build routes

### 1. Paint Anchor

**Primary:** REB · IDEF · BLK · STR  
**Secondary:** FIN (enough to punish dumps) · CLU  
**Story:** End possessions and erase paint touches. The classic five.

Draft glass and interior D like your season depends on it — because it does.

### 2. Power Finisher

**Primary:** FIN · REB · STR · DNK  
**Secondary:** IDEF · ATH · CLU  
**Risk:** Zero interior D turns wins into track meets against drivers

You score at the rim and on the offensive glass. Keep enough IDEF that the other team cannot ignore you defensively.

### 3. Playmaking Stretch Five

**Primary:** PAS · 3PT or MID · REB  
**Secondary:** IDEF · HAN crumbs · CLU  
**Rarity:** Hardest route; Chaos-friendly because skills travel

This is the unicorn / hub-big fantasy — creation from the elbow and arc. Do not attempt it until you have survived Anchor drafts. Empty REB still fails even if PAS looks pretty.

## Draft phases

| Phase | Goal                                                      |
| ----- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1–4   | REB + IDEF foundation                                     |
| 5–9   | Choose anchor vs finisher vs stretch-passer               |
| 10–13 | STR/BLK/FIN glue; specialty 3PT/PAS only after foundation |

## Mode notes

- **Classic:** Take the boring REB lock every time it appears early.
- **Blind:** You know you are a C — draft anchors and finishing bigs by role tags.
- **Chaos:** REB+IDEF+FIN survives SG/SF/PF reveals better than 3PT-only unicorn plans.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                        | Fix                           |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| PAS/3PT before REB             | Foundation first              |
| BLK as your only defense       | Pair with IDEF                |
| Ignoring FIN on anchors        | You still need to score dumps |
| Treating DNK as a full offense | Add FIN/STR                   |

## Sample Classic lock order (Paint Anchor)

1. **REB**
2. **IDEF**
3. **BLK** or **STR**
4. **FIN** (enough to punish dump-offs)
5. **CLU**
   6–13. DNK/ATH spice; **3PT/PAS** only after the foundation would start for a real team

Playmaking Stretch Five is a late-game unlock for experienced players. Attempting PAS/3PT before REB is how unicorn dreams become 30-win seasons.

## Season checklist after reveal

- Glass: are you top-tier on boards, or merely tall?
- Rim protection: IDEF+BLK changing shot diets, or only collecting highlight blocks?
- Finishing: can you convert the easy ones your guards create?
- Specialty fives: does creation/spacing appear _without_ abandoning the paint job?

Centers who skip FIN become brick monuments. Centers who skip REB become tall wings. Neither wins cleanly over 82.

## Why Chaos still wants REB first

Hidden position makes specialty skills risky, but **REB** and **IDEF** remain useful if you reveal as PF — and still matter if you somehow land as a oversized wing who crashes. That portability is why Anchor habits transfer better than Stretch-Five gambling.
