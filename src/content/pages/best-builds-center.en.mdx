A strong **Build a Hooper center build** begins with the paint and the glass. Centers need REB to end possessions, IDEF to control the interior, and enough BLK, FIN, or STR to define whether the player becomes a Paint Anchor, Power Finisher, or specialty Playmaking Stretch Five. The position can add shooting or passing, but those skills should not replace the foundation.

This guide explains the best Build a Hooper center build priorities, three reliable archetypes, draft phases, Classic, Blind, and Chaos adjustments, common mistakes, and season checkpoints. ## Build a Hooper center build quick answer

For a dependable center, prioritize:

1. **REB** — finish defensive possessions and create second chances
2. **IDEF** — protect the paint and survive post matchups
3. **BLK or FIN** — choose defense-first or scoring-first direction
4. **STR** — improve positioning, contact, and interior control
5. **ATH, DNK, CLU, PAS, or 3PT** — add route-specific support

The safest Build a Hooper center build secures REB + IDEF before round seven, then adds BLK/STR for a Paint Anchor or FIN/STR for a Power Finisher. Specialty creation and shooting come after the center can already perform the basic job.

## What a center must do

A center has four central responsibilities.

### End possessions

REB is the first priority in most Build a Hooper center build plans. Good defense is incomplete if the opponent collects the miss. REB also creates offensive putbacks and gives the player value without needing plays called for them.

### Protect the paint

IDEF represents positioning, post resistance, help defense, and deterrence. BLK creates visible rim-protection events, but IDEF provides the more consistent base. The strongest center defenses normally use IDEF + REB, then add BLK and STR.

### Convert interior chances

FIN measures touch and conversion around the rim. DNK adds above-the-rim power, while STR helps the player create and absorb contact. A defensive center still needs enough FIN to punish dump-offs and offensive rebounds.

### Add a specialty only after the foundation

PAS can create a hub-big route. 3PT or MID can create a stretch center. ATH can create a mobile rim runner. The best Build a Hooper center build adds those specialties after REB and IDEF are secure.

## Route 1: Paint Anchor

The Paint Anchor is the safest and most consistent Build a Hooper center build.

**Primary attributes:** REB, IDEF, BLK, STR  
**Secondary attributes:** FIN, CLU, ATH  
**Optional support:** DNK, PAS  
**Avoid early:** 3PT or HAN if the defensive foundation is incomplete

This route ends possessions, protects the rim, and controls physical matchups. It does not need elite scoring, but enough FIN prevents the defense from ignoring the center on dump-offs and offensive rebounds.

### Paint Anchor draft plan

- **Rounds 1–4:** secure REB and IDEF.
- **Rounds 5–7:** add BLK and STR.
- **Rounds 8–10:** add FIN and ATH.
- **Rounds 11–13:** complete CLU, DNK, light PAS, or remaining support.

A Paint Anchor with BLK but weak REB may create highlights and still lose the possession. A Paint Anchor with defense but no FIN can become a zero on easy offensive chances.

## Route 2: Power Finisher

The Power Finisher is an offense-first Build a Hooper center build that still preserves enough paint defense.

**Primary attributes:** FIN, REB, STR, DNK  
**Secondary attributes:** IDEF, ATH, CLU  
**Optional support:** BLK, MID  
**Main risk:** creating a scorer who gives every point back on defense

This route scores at the rim, controls contact, and creates second chances. FIN is more important than DNK because routine touch matters on more possessions. REB keeps the player involved when the initial attempt misses.

### Power Finisher draft plan

Take REB and FIN early, then add STR and IDEF. Add DNK and ATH once the interior scoring path already works. Use BLK and CLU as supporting ratings rather than replacing the basic package.

The best Build a Hooper center build in this route remains a credible defender even when scoring is the headline skill.

## Route 3: Playmaking Stretch Five

The Playmaking Stretch Five is the most difficult Build a Hooper center build.

**Primary attributes:** REB, PAS, 3PT or MID  
**Secondary attributes:** IDEF, FIN, HAN, CLU  
**Optional support:** STR, ATH  
**Identity check:** specialty creation does not excuse weak REB or IDEF

This route creates from the elbow or arc, spaces the floor, and functions as an offensive hub. It requires more successful ratings than the traditional routes because the center still needs enough interior value to remain playable.

### Playmaking Stretch Five draft plan

- Secure REB in the first four rounds.
- Add IDEF before the middle of the draft.
- Take PAS and 3PT/MID when strong donors appear.
- Add FIN or STR so the player can punish smaller matchups.
- Use HAN and CLU as late support.

Attempt this route in Classic before trying it in Blind or Chaos. Visible grades and three rerolls make the difficult combination more realistic.

## Choosing between BLK, FIN, and specialty skills

### BLK-first center

BLK-first builds should become Paint Anchors. Pair BLK with IDEF and REB immediately. Add STR and FIN so the player can hold position and convert easy chances.

### FIN-first center

FIN-first builds should become Power Finishers or Rim Runners. Pair FIN with REB, STR, and IDEF. Add DNK or ATH after the basic interior package is secure.

### PAS-first center

PAS-first builds are risky. Add REB and IDEF quickly, then decide whether the player is a hub big or whether passing should become a secondary tool. PAS without a scoring or rebounding foundation creates a specialty card with no center job.

### 3PT-first center

A stretch center still needs REB and IDEF. Add FIN or STR to punish closeouts and mismatches. If the draft refuses the big-man foundation, consider whether the player is becoming a PF rather than a C.

## Center draft phases

### Rounds 1–4: establish the foundation

Take REB and begin IDEF. If an elite FIN or BLK donor appears, take it, but do not let both foundation ratings remain empty. By round four, the Build a Hooper center build should already resemble a big.

### Rounds 5–9: choose the route

Add BLK/STR for a Paint Anchor, FIN/STR/DNK for a Power Finisher, or PAS/3PT for a specialty route after REB and IDEF exist. This phase determines whether the player has one complete job or several unfinished ideas.

### Rounds 10–13: close the gaps

Use the final picks for ATH, CLU, FIN, STR, BLK, secondary scoring, or specialty creation. Do not begin a guard-style handle package late unless the rest of the center is already complete.

## Classic mode center strategy

Classic is the best mode for a precise Build a Hooper center build because grades are visible and three rerolls are available. The main Classic trap is chasing specialty ratings because their letters look exciting. A visible A+ 3PT can distract from weak REB and IDEF.

Save one reroll for a later board that cannot support the remaining center responsibilities. Use rerolls when no useful rebounder, interior defender, finisher, or route-compatible player appears. Do not reroll a connected B+ only to hunt a theoretical A+.

Classic is also the best environment for testing the Playmaking Stretch Five because the route needs more precise grade control than Paint Anchor or Power Finisher.

## Blind mode center strategy

Blind reveals C but hides ratings. Prefer players known for rebounding, rim protection, physical finishing, passing as a big, or stretch-center play. Because the position is known, REB and IDEF remain the safest early targets.

Keep two related routes open. A planned Paint Anchor can become a Power Finisher when FIN and STR dominate the draft. A stretch plan can become an Anchor when shooting fails but REB, IDEF, and BLK appear. The Build a Hooper center build should pivot among center roles rather than abandon the paint.

## Chaos mode center strategy

Chaos does not guarantee a center reveal. A pure REB/IDEF/BLK/STR package may become awkward at PG or SG, while a pure 3PT/PAS plan may fail badly if the player reveals as C. Favor portable combinations such as REB + FIN + IDEF, then add ATH, PDEF, 3PT, or HAN according to the board.

The safest Chaos Build a Hooper center build base is not a perfect center specialist. It is a player with enough rebounding and finishing to survive PF/C outcomes while retaining athletic, defensive, or shooting value for wings.

## Common center build mistakes

### Taking specialty skills before REB

PAS and 3PT can create exciting center routes, but a center still needs to end possessions. REB should normally appear early.

### Treating BLK as the entire defense

BLK should complement IDEF and REB. A block does not matter if the player loses position or gives up the offensive rebound.

### Ignoring FIN on a defensive center

Even a Paint Anchor receives dump-offs, putbacks, and short interior attempts. Enough FIN keeps the player from becoming an offensive liability.

### Treating DNK as a complete scoring package

DNK needs FIN, STR, and ATH. The highlight finish is not the same as reliable interior offense.

### Building a Stretch Five with no paint value

3PT alone does not create a center. Add REB, IDEF, and enough FIN or STR to survive inside.

### Copying PF priorities without enough rim protection

PF and C overlap, but the center often carries more responsibility for the paint and glass. IDEF, BLK, REB, and STR deserve more attention.

## Season checklist for a center

After the reveal and during the 82-game season, ask:

- Is REB consistently ending defensive possessions?
- Does IDEF deter paint attacks?
- Does BLK support the defense without replacing positioning?
- Can the center convert easy interior chances through FIN?
- Does STR improve contact and post control?
- If the player is a Power Finisher, do ATH and DNK create reliable pressure?
- If the player is a Stretch Five, does shooting create spacing without sacrificing the glass?
- If the player is a hub big, do PAS and HAN create real offense?
- Is the player still effective after game 50 and in playoff series?

A successful Build a Hooper center build should provide reliable value even when the specialty skill has a quiet night because rebounding and interior defense remain available.

## When to pivot during the draft

Pivot from Paint Anchor to Power Finisher when FIN, STR, and DNK become unusually strong while REB and IDEF are already secure. Pivot from Power Finisher to Anchor when BLK and IDEF dominate the middle rounds. Pivot toward Playmaking Stretch Five only when REB exists and PAS plus 3PT/MID develop early enough to complete the route.

A good pivot keeps the center foundation. A bad pivot abandons REB and IDEF after round nine.

## Build a Hooper center build FAQ

### What is the most important center attribute?

REB is the safest first priority, followed by IDEF. Together they perform the most consistent center responsibilities.

### Is BLK more important than IDEF?

Usually no. IDEF provides consistent positioning and deterrence, while BLK adds events. The best center defense uses both after REB is secure.

### Does every center need FIN?

Every center benefits from enough FIN to convert dump-offs, rolls, and putbacks. A defensive anchor can use a lower grade than a Power Finisher, but it should not be ignored.

### Can a center be a shooter?

Yes. A Stretch Five can use 3PT or MID, but REB and IDEF should remain part of the core.

### What is the easiest center route?

Paint Anchor is the clearest: REB + IDEF, then BLK + STR, followed by FIN, ATH, and CLU.

### What is the safest Chaos center base?

REB + FIN + IDEF, followed by ATH or PDEF, travels better across PF/C and some wing outcomes than a narrow center specialist.
