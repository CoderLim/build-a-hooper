Shooting guards usually begin with 3PT or MID, add FIN or a second scoring level, and secure enough HAN to attack closeouts. PDEF, ATH, and CLU can turn the scorer into a stable two-way player over an 82-game season.

## Build a Hooper shooting guard build quick answer

For a dependable SG, prioritize:

1. **3PT or MID** — establish a primary scoring axis
2. **FIN** — punish hard closeouts and create rim pressure
3. **HAN** — keep the player from becoming catch-and-shoot only
4. **ATH or CLU** — improve movement, burst, and late-game value
5. **PDEF** — create a two-way route and avoid defensive targeting

The safest Build a Hooper shooting guard build has at least two credible scoring responses. That could be 3PT + FIN, 3PT + MID, or MID + FIN with enough HAN to create the shot.

## What a shooting guard must do

A shooting guard has four main jobs.

### Create scoring gravity

The defense should respect the player before the ball arrives. 3PT creates spacing and off-ball gravity. MID creates a pull-up and half-court counter. A Build a Hooper shooting guard build without a real scoring axis does not perform the position’s basic role.

### Punish the closeout

FIN and HAN become important once defenders run the player off the line. A shooter with no handle or finishing tool can be neutralized by aggressive perimeter pressure. Even a movement-shooter route needs enough HAN to take one or two dribbles into a useful second action.

### Contribute without the ball

ATH supports relocation, transition, cuts, and recovery. PDEF gives the player value when shots are not falling. REB can help a larger wing, but it is usually secondary to scoring and perimeter defense.

### Close possessions

CLU is useful for late-game shot-making, but it should strengthen an existing scoring identity. It should not replace 3PT, MID, FIN, or HAN.

## Route 1: Movement Shooter

The Movement Shooter is a spacing-first Build a Hooper shooting guard build.

**Primary attributes:** 3PT, ATH, enough HAN  
**Secondary attributes:** MID, CLU, PDEF  
**Optional support:** FIN, PAS  
**Avoid early:** BLK, IDEF, heavy REB

This route creates gravity through shooting and movement. The player should be dangerous on the catch, while HAN and FIN provide an answer when defenders overplay the arc.

### Movement Shooter draft plan

- **Rounds 1–4:** secure 3PT and begin ATH or HAN.
- **Rounds 5–7:** add MID or FIN as the second scoring response.
- **Rounds 8–10:** add PDEF or CLU.
- **Rounds 11–13:** complete HAN, secondary scoring, or light PAS.

A Movement Shooter with elite 3PT but no HAN is too dependent on clean catches. A Movement Shooter with ATH but weak shooting is simply athletic, not a shooter.

## Route 2: Three-Level Scorer

The Three-Level Scorer is the most complete offensive Build a Hooper shooting guard build.

**Primary attributes:** 3PT, MID, FIN  
**Secondary attributes:** HAN, ATH, CLU  
**Optional support:** PDEF, PAS  
**Main risk:** spreading early picks too widely before one scoring level becomes reliable

This route can score beyond the arc, from the middle, and at the rim. It is powerful in Classic because visible ratings allow precise planning, but it can become expensive. The build still needs HAN, or the player will not consistently access all three levels.

### Three-Level Scorer draft plan

Start with the strongest available scoring axis. Add the second level before round seven. Take HAN as soon as a useful donor appears. FIN should be complete before the late rounds because it prevents the player from becoming jump-shot dependent. Use CLU and ATH as finishing tools rather than early foundations.

## Route 3: Two-Way Scorer

The Two-Way Scorer combines a reliable scoring path with perimeter defense.

**Primary attributes:** 3PT or MID, PDEF, FIN or HAN  
**Secondary attributes:** ATH, CLU  
**Optional support:** REB, STR, PAS  
**Identity check:** weak PDEF means the route is not truly two-way

The Build a Hooper shooting guard build in this route may score slightly less than a pure Three-Level Scorer, but it offers more stable season and playoff value. PDEF and ATH help against opposing guards and wings, while FIN or HAN preserves offensive flexibility.

### Two-Way Scorer draft plan

- Establish 3PT or MID in the first four rounds.
- Take elite PDEF when it appears without abandoning scoring.
- Add FIN or HAN before round nine.
- Finish with ATH, CLU, or secondary shooting.

Do not overcorrect toward defense. A two-way shooting guard still needs to score.

## Choosing the scoring axis

### 3PT-first SG

This is the default modern route. 3PT creates spacing and works well with ATH, HAN, and PDEF. Pair it with FIN or MID so the player survives hard closeouts.

### MID-first SG

MID can work when the board refuses 3PT or when you are building a controlled half-court scorer. Pair it with FIN and HAN. Add enough 3PT to prevent defenders from ignoring the arc if the draft allows.

### FIN-first SG

FIN-first builds should become slashers or physical scoring guards. Add ATH and HAN quickly, then install 3PT or MID before the paint becomes crowded. DNK can support the route, but FIN is the more important foundation.

### Balanced scoring SG

The strongest Build a Hooper shooting guard build may finish with solid grades across 3PT, MID, FIN, and HAN rather than one extreme rating. Balance becomes especially useful in Blind and Chaos, where precision is harder.

## SG draft phases

### Rounds 1–4: establish scoring

Lock 3PT or MID early. If FIN is exceptional, take it as the start of a slashing route, but add a jump-shot threat soon. By round four, the player should have a clear offensive direction.

### Rounds 5–9: build the second response

Add FIN, HAN, or the second shooting level. Decide whether PDEF will make the build two-way. This phase should prevent the SG from becoming one-dimensional.

### Rounds 10–13: add stability

Use the final picks for ATH, CLU, PDEF, secondary HAN, PAS, REB, or STR. Do not begin a center-style defensive package late in the draft.

## Classic mode SG strategy

Classic is ideal for a precise Build a Hooper shooting guard build because grades are visible and three rerolls are available. Choose the route before the first spin and use visible ratings to build connected scoring skills.

Use a reroll when the roster offers no shooting or finishing option compatible with the chosen identity. Do not spend all three rerolls before round six. The later rounds may require a specific HAN, FIN, or PDEF repair.

Classic is also the best environment for comparing a Movement Shooter against a Three-Level Scorer. Repeat the position with different priority orders and compare overall rating, regular-season stability, and playoff results.

## Blind mode SG strategy

Blind reveals SG but hides ratings. Prefer players known for shooting, scoring, wing defense, or secondary creation. Do not assume every famous scorer has the exact 3PT or MID grade you want; treat player role as evidence rather than certainty.

Build a flexible scoring profile. If the draft repeatedly offers finishers and athletes, pivot from Movement Shooter to Slashing or Three-Level. If strong defensive wings appear, pivot toward Two-Way Scorer. The Build a Hooper shooting guard build should remain an SG even when the exact grades are unknown.

## Chaos mode SG strategy

Chaos can reveal any position, so a pure 3PT specialist becomes risky. Pair shooting with FIN, ATH, PDEF, or REB so the player can survive a wing or forward reveal. HAN + 3PT + FIN is useful for guard outcomes, while 3PT + PDEF + REB travels better toward SF or PF.

The goal in Chaos is not the perfect Build a Hooper shooting guard build. It is a versatile player that still has a scoring identity if the final position changes.

## Common SG build mistakes

### Taking DNK before the scoring foundation

DNK is exciting, but it does not replace FIN, 3PT, MID, or HAN. Build the path to the rim before adding the highlight finish.

### Building a shooter with zero HAN

Defenders will run the player off the arc. Enough HAN is required to attack the closeout and reach the second scoring option.

### Taking all three scoring ratings without creation

3PT, MID, and FIN look complete, but weak HAN can make the player dependent on assisted opportunities. A Three-Level Scorer still needs access to the three levels.

### Ignoring PDEF on a “two-way” label

ATH alone does not create perimeter defense. PDEF must be meaningful if the route is called Two-Way Scorer.

### Copying point guard priorities

A normal SG does not need PAS before the scoring axis. Secondary passing is useful, but HAN and scoring usually come first.

### Chasing overall instead of a shot profile

A high overall rating can hide a player with no dependable scoring sequence. Read the [attribute guide](/attributes) and final sheet before judging the build.

## Season checklist for an SG

After the reveal and during the 82-game season, ask:

- Does the defense respect the primary scoring axis?
- Can the SG attack when defenders remove the first shot?
- Is FIN converting closeouts into points?
- Does HAN create useful second actions?
- Is PDEF strong enough to avoid becoming a playoff target?
- Does ATH improve movement and transition rather than exist as an isolated grade?
- Does CLU strengthen a real shot profile?
- Is the player producing consistently after game 40?

A successful Build a Hooper shooting guard build should create points in more than one way and provide enough secondary value to survive cold shooting nights.

## When to pivot during the draft

Pivot from Movement Shooter to Three-Level Scorer when MID and FIN keep appearing while 3PT is already secure. Pivot from any scorer to Two-Way Scorer when elite PDEF appears before round seven. Pivot from an SG plan toward an SF-style wing only when REB, PDEF, ATH, and size-related ratings develop while pure guard creation remains weak.

A good pivot uses the same scoring foundation. A bad pivot starts a completely unrelated role after round nine.

## Build a Hooper shooting guard build FAQ

### What is the most important SG attribute?

Usually 3PT or MID, because the position needs a primary scoring axis. FIN and HAN become essential support ratings.

### Does every SG need 3PT?

No. A MID + FIN scorer can work, but some 3PT helps spacing. A complete lack of outside threat can make the player easier to defend.

### How much HAN should a shooter have?

Enough to attack closeouts and create a second shot. A pure off-ball route needs less HAN than a Shot Creator, but it should not be ignored.

### Is PDEF worth an early pick?

Yes for a deliberate Two-Way Scorer route, especially if the scoring axis is already secure. Do not take defense so early that the player has no offense.

### What is the easiest SG route?

Movement Shooter is easy to understand: 3PT first, then enough HAN, FIN or MID, ATH, and PDEF. Three-Level Scorer requires more successful scoring picks.

### What is the safest Chaos SG base?

3PT + FIN + PDEF or 3PT + ATH + REB travels better across guard, wing, and forward outcomes than pure shooting alone.
