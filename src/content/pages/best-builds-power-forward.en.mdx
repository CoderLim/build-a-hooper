# Best Power Forward Build

Power forward sits between wing skill and center size. Traditional primers emphasize rebounding, interior defense, and finishing; modern lineups also ask many fours to space ([Nike on evolving bigs](https://www.nike.com/a/positions-in-basketball), [position skill guides](https://halloffame-online.com/blog/basketball-positions-explained-roles-responsibilities-skills/)). Build a Hooper PF cards usually live or die on **REB**, **IDEF**, and either **FIN/STR** (enforcer) or **3PT** (stretch).

## Position identity

A PF who cannot rebound is a big wing without the wing tools. A PF who cannot protect the paint or space the floor becomes a crowded-lane liability. Pick one clear offensive job, then secure the glass.

## Priority order (Classic)

1. **REB**
2. **IDEF**
3. **FIN** or **STR** (enforcer) _or_ **3PT** (stretch)
4. **BLK** / **MID**
5. **ATH** / **CLU**

## Three build routes

### 1. Interior Enforcer

**Primary:** REB · IDEF · FIN · STR  
**Secondary:** BLK · CLU · light MID  
**Skip early:** Pure 3PT chasing if it costs glass

You bully the paint on both ends. This is the traditional four. Seasons reward the boring REB locks you almost skipped.

### 2. Stretch Four

**Primary:** 3PT · REB · IDEF  
**Secondary:** MID · FIN · CLU  
**Risk:** Empty IDEF + empty REB = “shooter” who loses every possession

Spacing is the point — not an excuse to abandon the glass. Stretch bigs in real lineups still need to end trips and rotate.

### 3. Rim Runner

**Primary:** FIN · ATH · DNK · REB  
**Secondary:** IDEF crumbs · STR · CLU  
**Partner dependency:** You want to finish lobs and rolls; keep REB so misses come back

Explosive fours. Pair with enough IDEF that you are not a cone when the opponent posts.

## Draft phases

| Phase | Goal                                    |
| ----- | --------------------------------------- |
| 1–4   | REB first; start IDEF                   |
| 5–9   | Commit enforcer vs stretch vs runner    |
| 10–13 | BLK/CLU/ATH glue; never leave REB empty |

## Mode notes

- **Classic:** Easy to overdraft offense; force yourself to take REB/IDEF early.
- **Blind:** Prefer known glass eaters and stretch bigs by role.
- **Chaos:** REB + FIN is the safest PF/C insurance pair if the reveal slides to center.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                       | Fix                                            |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| 3PT before REB on “stretch”   | Glass first, then spacing                      |
| BLK without IDEF/REB          | Blocks ≠ paint kit                             |
| Ignoring FIN on enforcers     | Strength without touch still misses            |
| Building a wing with PF label | If PDEF/3PT dominate and REB is empty, play SF |

## Sample Classic lock order (Interior Enforcer)

1. **REB** (non-negotiable early)
2. **IDEF**
3. **FIN** or **STR** — whichever grades higher on a true big
4. **BLK** if available without skipping glass
5. **CLU** / **MID** crumbs
   6–13. ATH, light 3PT only after the paint kit is real

Stretch Four swaps step 3 toward **3PT**, but steps 1–2 stay identical. If you cannot say “I rebound,” you do not have a stretch four — you have a misplaced wing.

## Season checklist after reveal

- Opponent second-chance rate: is your REB actually ending trips?
- Paint deterrence: IDEF/BLK showing up, or are drivers living rent-free?
- Stretch fours: does your 3PT create drive lanes, or is spacing fake?

Enforcer cards that skip FIN look tough in the draft room and soft on putbacks. Fix it before you blame seeding.

## Blind / Chaos note

When grades hide, prefer known glass-first forwards and finishing bigs. A “stretch” reputation without REB insurance is how Chaos reveals into a center-shaped hole. Pair **REB + FIN** as the portable PF/C kit.
