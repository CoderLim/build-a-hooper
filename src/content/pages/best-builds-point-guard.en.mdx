A strong **Build a Hooper point guard build** starts with one question: can this player control an offense for an entire season? Point guards need enough ball security to survive pressure, enough passing to create advantages, and enough scoring to stop defenders from ignoring them. The exact route can be pass-first, score-first, or two-way, but a PG build should never look like a shooting guard card with a point guard label.

This guide explains the best Build a Hooper point guard build priorities, three reliable archetypes, round-by-round decisions, Classic, Blind, and Chaos adjustments, common mistakes, and season checkpoints. Review the [complete attribute guide](/attributes) for every rating definition and the [best builds hub](/best-builds) for other positions.

## Build a Hooper point guard build quick answer

For a dependable PG, prioritize:

1. **HAN** — handles and ball security
2. **PAS** — passing and playmaking
3. **3PT or MID** — one reliable scoring axis
4. **FIN or CLU** — a second response and late-game value
5. **PDEF or ATH** — enough two-way support to survive matchups

The safest Build a Hooper point guard build secures HAN and PAS before round seven, installs a real scoring threat before round ten, and uses the final picks to cover defense, athleticism, finishing, or clutch play.

## What a point guard must do

A point guard has three non-negotiable jobs.

### Protect the ball

HAN is the foundation of nearly every Build a Hooper point guard build. It represents live-dribble control, pressure resistance, escape moves, and the ability to initiate offense without turning the ball over. High shooting with weak HAN produces a player who depends on someone else to create the shot.

### Create an advantage

PAS turns ball control into team offense. It covers decision speed, skip passes, pocket feeds, and the ability to punish defensive help. A PG with HAN but no PAS can become an isolation scorer. That can work as a deliberate route, but it is not a traditional floor general.

### Remain a scoring threat

The defense must respect one scoring level. 3PT provides spacing and pull-up gravity. MID provides a half-court counter. FIN punishes switches and closeouts. The best Build a Hooper point guard build usually has one primary scoring axis and one secondary response.

## Route 1: Floor General

The Floor General is the safest Build a Hooper point guard build for beginners and long seasons.

**Primary attributes:** HAN, PAS  
**Secondary attributes:** 3PT or MID, CLU, PDEF  
**Optional support:** FIN, ATH  
**Avoid early:** DNK, BLK, REB, IDEF

The Floor General controls tempo and creates for teammates. The player does not need to lead every scoring category, but the defense must respect the shot enough to prevent total pressure on passing lanes.

### Floor General draft plan

- **Rounds 1–4:** secure HAN and begin PAS.
- **Rounds 5–7:** complete HAN + PAS and add 3PT or MID.
- **Rounds 8–10:** add FIN or CLU.
- **Rounds 11–13:** cover PDEF, ATH, or another scoring level.

A Floor General with elite PAS and weak HAN is incomplete because passing opportunities require the ability to reach advantage positions. A Floor General with elite creation and no scoring threat can still struggle when defenders stop respecting the ball handler.

## Route 2: Shot Creator

The Shot Creator is a score-first Build a Hooper point guard build that uses HAN to produce pull-ups, drives, and late-clock offense.

**Primary attributes:** HAN, 3PT or MID, FIN  
**Secondary attributes:** PAS, ATH, CLU  
**Optional support:** PDEF  
**Main risk:** starving PAS until the player becomes an isolation-only guard

### Shot Creator draft plan

Secure HAN first, then choose a scoring axis. If 3PT appears early, pair it with FIN or MID. If MID appears early, pair it with FIN and enough 3PT to keep the floor open when possible. Take PAS as soon as a useful donor appears; do not wait until the final round.

The best Build a Hooper point guard build in this route still creates for teammates. Secondary PAS is enough, but completely empty passing makes every difficult matchup harder.

## Route 3: Two-Way Lead Guard

The Two-Way Lead Guard combines creation with perimeter defense.

**Primary attributes:** HAN, PDEF, PAS or 3PT  
**Secondary attributes:** ATH, FIN, CLU  
**Optional support:** MID  
**Identity check:** if PDEF is weak, this is not a two-way route

This Build a Hooper point guard build is difficult because the draft must support offense and defense early. It becomes valuable over 82 games because the player can start offense and avoid becoming a target at the other end.

### Two-Way Lead Guard draft plan

- Take HAN in the first four rounds.
- Take PDEF or ATH when an elite defensive donor appears.
- Choose PAS for a true organizer or 3PT for a scoring lead guard.
- Add FIN and CLU after the core three ratings are secure.

Do not sacrifice all scoring to chase defense. A two-way guard must still create enough offensive value to justify the role.

## Choosing between 3PT, MID, and FIN

A Build a Hooper point guard build does not need every scoring rating at an elite level, but it needs a coherent combination.

### 3PT-first PG

Best for spacing, pull-up gravity, and score-first guards. Pair 3PT with HAN and either PAS or FIN. A 3PT-first guard with no finishing can become too easy to chase off the line.

### MID-first PG

Best for controlled half-court scoring and a fallback when the draft does not offer threes. Pair MID with HAN, PAS, and FIN. Add some 3PT if possible so defenders cannot ignore the arc.

### FIN-first PG

Best for athletic slashers and pressure guards. FIN needs HAN and ATH to create access to the rim. Add 3PT or MID so the paint does not become crowded for the entire season.

The strongest Build a Hooper point guard build usually combines one primary scoring choice with one secondary response instead of spreading early picks across all four scoring attributes.

## PG draft phases

### Rounds 1–4: build the engine

Take HAN early. Add PAS for a Floor General, a scoring axis for a Shot Creator, or PDEF for a Two-Way Lead Guard. By round four, the Build a Hooper point guard build should have a direction.

### Rounds 5–9: complete creation and scoring

This phase should produce a functioning offense. Do not leave both PAS and HAN weak. Add 3PT, MID, or FIN so the player can punish the way defenders guard the primary action.

### Rounds 10–13: add stability

Use the final picks for PDEF, ATH, CLU, secondary PAS, or another scoring level. Ignore big-man ratings unless every guard responsibility is already secure or the mode demands positional insurance.

## Classic mode PG strategy

Classic is the easiest mode for a precise Build a Hooper point guard build because position and grades are visible and three rerolls are available.

Use rerolls when a roster offers no useful creation or scoring donor, not simply because the visible grade is below A. Save one reroll for the second half of the draft. Classic also lets you test one archetype repeatedly, compare different lock orders, and learn whether the season prefers more passing, more scoring, or more defense for your chosen route.

## Blind mode PG strategy

Blind reveals PG but hides ratings. Draft known creators, lead guards, and scoring guards according to role rather than assumed exact grades. Because the position is known, HAN and PAS remain the safest early priorities.

Keep a backup identity. If the roster repeatedly offers scoring guards and refuses playmakers, a Floor General plan can become a Shot Creator with secondary PAS. The Build a Hooper point guard build should pivot between neighboring guard roles rather than abandoning creation entirely.

## Chaos mode PG strategy

Chaos does not guarantee a PG reveal. A pure PG plan built only around HAN and PAS can become awkward if the player reveals as a center or power forward. Favor portable combinations such as HAN + FIN + PDEF or HAN + 3PT + ATH, then add REB or IDEF insurance if the board strongly suggests a larger player.

The goal in Chaos is not the perfect Build a Hooper point guard build. The goal is a player that can still function if the position is not PG.

## Common PG build mistakes

### Taking 3PT before HAN every time

A great shot does not matter if the player cannot reliably create or protect the possession. HAN normally comes first unless the 3PT opportunity is exceptional and handles are likely to appear soon.

### Ignoring PAS on a score-first guard

Shot Creator does not mean zero passing. Secondary PAS helps punish double teams and keeps the offense from becoming predictable.

### Taking CLU too early

CLU improves a finished identity. It does not create one. Build HAN, PAS, and the scoring axis before treating clutch as a priority.

### Calling high PDEF a two-way build without offense

A defensive guard still needs creation and enough scoring to stay useful. Two-way means both sides, not defense plus an empty offensive sheet.

### Chasing big-man ratings

REB, IDEF, and BLK can be fun, but they rarely improve a normal PG more than HAN, PAS, scoring, PDEF, ATH, or CLU.

### Using overall rating as the role

A high overall PG can still fail if creation is weak or the scoring profile is one-dimensional. Read the [attribute guide](/attributes) and season results together.

## Season checklist for a PG

After the reveal and during the 82-game season, ask:

- Is the player protecting possessions?
- Does PAS create team offense, or is it only a decorative grade?
- Can the PG score when defenders take away the first option?
- Does FIN punish closeouts?
- Is PDEF strong enough to avoid becoming a matchup target?
- Does CLU improve late games after the offense already works?
- Is the record stable through games 40–60, or dependent on hot shooting?

A successful Build a Hooper point guard build should remain useful when the shot is not falling because creation, passing, and secondary skills still help the team.

## When to pivot during the draft

Pivot from Floor General to Shot Creator when shooting and FIN appear early but PAS repeatedly stalls. Pivot from Shot Creator to Two-Way Lead Guard when elite PDEF arrives and HAN is already strong. Do not pivot into a center-style rebounder or paint anchor unless Chaos position risk and the available ratings clearly demand it.

A good pivot reuses two or three previous locks. A bad pivot starts a new identity after round nine.

## Build a Hooper point guard build FAQ

### What is the most important PG attribute?

HAN is usually the first priority because it supports every creation route. PAS becomes equally important for a traditional Floor General.

### Is 3PT required for a point guard?

No, but the player needs a credible scoring threat. MID + FIN can work when 3PT is unavailable, though some outside spacing is helpful.

### How much PAS does a scoring PG need?

Enough to punish help defense and avoid becoming an isolation-only player. It does not need to match a Floor General, but it should not be ignored.

### Should a PG take PDEF?

Yes, after the offensive engine is secure. PDEF and ATH can turn a good offensive PG into a stable two-way player.

### What is the easiest PG route?

The Floor General is the easiest to understand because HAN and PAS provide a clear foundation, followed by one scoring axis.

### What is the safest Chaos guard base?

HAN + FIN + PDEF or HAN + 3PT + ATH travels better across guard and wing outcomes than a narrow PAS-only build.
