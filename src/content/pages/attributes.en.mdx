The **Build a Hooper attributes** system is the foundation of every run. No matter which [game mode](/modes) you choose or which position you reveal, your final player is built from the same thirteen ratings: **3PT, MID, FIN, DNK, HAN, PAS, PDEF, IDEF, BLK, REB, ATH, STR, and CLU**. The challenge is not simply collecting the highest letter grades. The challenge is combining the right Build a Hooper attributes so the player has a clear basketball job across an entire 82-game season.

This guide explains what every rating means, which Build a Hooper attributes belong together, how priorities change by position, and when a strong grade is actually a bad pick. New players should also read the [complete beginner guide](/how-to-play), while experienced players can jump to the [best builds hub](/best-builds) for position-specific plans.

## Build a Hooper attributes at a glance

| Cluster | Attributes | Main purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scoring | 3PT, MID, FIN, DNK | Create points from the arc, mid-range, rim, and above the rim |
| Creation | HAN, PAS | Protect the ball and create advantages for teammates |
| Defense and rebounding | PDEF, IDEF, BLK, REB | Stop perimeter attacks, protect the paint, and finish possessions |
| Physical and late-game tools | ATH, STR, CLU | Add speed, force, contact resistance, and closing ability |

A good draft usually builds one primary cluster, supports it with a secondary cluster, and avoids leaving a position-critical rating empty. Build a Hooper attributes work as a system: elite shooting is less useful without handles or finishing, while elite shot blocking is incomplete without interior defense and rebounding.

## How to read letter grades

Letter grades are a quick comparison tool, not a promise that one A+ will carry the season. An A in the wrong category can be less useful than a B+ that completes your role. Before every lock, ask three questions:

1. Does this Build a Hooper attribute support the role I am building?
2. Is the attribute required for my position every night?
3. Does this pick fix a weakness, or am I only chasing the best visible letter?

In [Classic mode](/modes), ratings are visible, so you can compare grades directly. In Blind and Chaos, the Build a Hooper attributes are hidden during the draft, which makes player role and roster context more important than letter hunting.

## Scoring Build a Hooper attributes

### 3PT — three-point shooting

**3PT** represents spacing, catch-and-shoot value, movement shooting, and the threat of pull-up threes. It is one of the most valuable Build a Hooper attributes for shooting guards, small forwards, scoring point guards, stretch power forwards, and modern centers.

A high 3PT grade forces defenders to respect the arc. That creates room for finishing and passing. However, 3PT alone does not create a complete scorer. A player with elite 3PT but weak HAN can struggle when defenders run them off the line. A player with elite 3PT but weak FIN may have no second option once the shot is denied.

**Draft priority:** high for SG and spacing wings; medium to high for PG, SF, and stretch bigs; optional for paint-focused centers.

### MID — mid-range scoring

**MID** covers pull-up jumpers, elbow shots, short fadeaways, and scoring between the rim and three-point line. Among the Build a Hooper attributes, MID is often underrated because players immediately chase 3PT. It becomes valuable when your build needs a second scoring level or when the board refuses to offer strong outside shooting.

MID pairs naturally with FIN for a physical wing or with 3PT for a three-level scorer. A pure MID build can still work, but it needs HAN, FIN, or CLU so it is not limited to one predictable shot area.

### FIN — finishing at the rim

**FIN** measures layups, floaters, touch, body control, and the ability to convert around the basket. FIN is one of the most transferable Build a Hooper attributes because every position benefits from making easy chances.

Guards need FIN to punish closeouts. Wings need it for cuts and transition. Bigs need it for rolls, putbacks, and interior touches. A common mistake is taking DNK before FIN. Dunking creates highlights, but finishing determines whether routine rim attempts become points.

**Draft priority:** useful for every position and especially important for slashers, scoring guards, power forwards, and centers.

### DNK — dunking and vertical finishing

**DNK** represents above-the-rim power, lobs, putback dunks, and explosive finishes. It is the flashiest of the scoring Build a Hooper attributes, but it should usually support FIN and ATH rather than replace them.

DNK is most effective for slashing wings, rim-running forwards, and athletic centers. It is less urgent for floor generals, movement shooters, and stretch bigs. Do not spend an early lock on DNK if your primary scoring skill, creation, or rebounding is still empty.

## Creation Build a Hooper attributes

### HAN — handles and ball security

**HAN** controls live-dribble creation, pressure resistance, direction changes, and the ability to start offense without losing the ball. For point guards, HAN is one of the most important Build a Hooper attributes in the entire game.

High shooting without HAN can create a player who scores only when someone else creates the opportunity. High FIN without HAN can produce a slasher who cannot consistently reach the rim. Shooting guards and point forwards also benefit from HAN because it lets them attack closeouts and create a second action.

**Draft priority:** essential for PG; high for shot-creating SG and point-forward SF; lower for traditional PF and C.

### PAS — passing and playmaking

**PAS** represents assist creation, decision speed, skip passes, pocket passes, and the ability to turn defensive pressure into open shots. PAS and HAN are complementary Build a Hooper attributes. HAN gets the player into advantage positions; PAS converts those advantages into team offense.

A floor-general point guard should secure both ratings. A scoring guard can survive with secondary PAS, while a playmaking center may prioritize PAS after REB and IDEF. PAS without HAN can still help an off-ball connector, but it rarely creates a complete lead guard.

## Defensive Build a Hooper attributes

### PDEF — perimeter defense

**PDEF** measures containment, screen navigation, closeouts, and the ability to defend guards and wings. It is a core Build a Hooper attribute for two-way shooting guards, small forwards, and defensive point guards.

PDEF becomes stronger when paired with ATH because athletic recovery helps the player stay attached after screens or direction changes. A 3-and-D wing should treat 3PT and PDEF as the identity pair, then add FIN, ATH, or REB as support.

### IDEF — interior defense

**IDEF** represents post resistance, paint positioning, help defense, and the ability to deter drives without relying on a block. It is one of the defining Build a Hooper attributes for power forwards and centers.

IDEF is different from BLK. A defender can protect the paint by forcing difficult attempts without recording blocks. For a dependable big, IDEF should usually come before specialty shooting or playmaking.

### BLK — shot blocking

**BLK** controls rim-protection events, recovery blocks, and help-side rejections. BLK is valuable, but it is not a complete defensive package by itself. The strongest interior Build a Hooper attributes combination is usually IDEF plus REB, with BLK added as a high-impact third tool.

A center with high BLK and weak REB may erase one shot and still lose the possession on the offensive rebound. A forward with high BLK and weak IDEF may chase highlights while giving up position.

### REB — rebounding

**REB** ends defensive possessions and creates extra offensive chances. It is the most important Build a Hooper attribute for many center and power-forward builds.

A center without REB is not performing the basic center job, regardless of overall rating. Power forwards usually need REB early, while small forwards can use a moderate grade to become stronger all-around wings. Guards should normally prioritize creation, scoring, and PDEF first unless Chaos mode requires more positional insurance.

## Physical and closing Build a Hooper attributes

### ATH — athleticism

**ATH** covers speed, first-step burst, recovery, transition movement, and functional vertical ability. ATH acts as a multiplier for several other Build a Hooper attributes. It helps FIN and DNK on offense, and it strengthens PDEF and BLK on defense.

ATH is especially useful for slashers, defensive wings, rim runners, and Chaos builds that need skills capable of surviving different position reveals. It should support a role rather than become the role by itself.

### STR — strength

**STR** represents contact balance, post leverage, screening force, interior positioning, and the ability to finish through physical defense. It is a high-value Build a Hooper attribute for PF and C, and it can also support physical wings.

STR pairs well with FIN, IDEF, and REB. A paint enforcer with weak STR may look complete on paper but fail to hold position. A perimeter shooter usually has more urgent priorities unless the rest of the build is already secure.

### CLU — clutch performance

**CLU** represents late-game execution, closing composure, and performance when possessions become more important. CLU is useful for every position, but it should rarely be the first Build a Hooper attribute you chase.

Clutch ability cannot repair a player with no reliable scoring path, no creation, or no defensive role. Build the identity first, then add CLU in the middle or late rounds. For a lead scorer or floor general, CLU is an excellent finishing touch once the primary cluster is complete.

## How modes change attribute strategy

The Build a Hooper attributes do not change between modes, but the information available during the draft does.

- **Classic:** grades are visible, so you can target specific ratings and repair weak clusters.
- **Blind:** your position is known, but ratings are hidden. Draft players whose known basketball roles match the Build a Hooper attributes your position needs.
- **Chaos:** position and ratings are hidden. Favor transferable combinations such as FIN + ATH, HAN + PDEF, or REB + IDEF rather than a narrow specialist build.

Read the full [Build a Hooper modes guide](/modes) before moving from Classic to Blind or Chaos.

## Common attribute mistakes

### Chasing the highest grade every round

The largest letter is not always the correct lock. A B+ PAS that completes a point guard can be more valuable than an A+ BLK that does not fit the role.

### Building a scorer with no second option

Pure 3PT, pure MID, or pure DNK builds become predictable. Add FIN, HAN, or another scoring level so the offense survives different matchups.

### Treating blocks as complete defense

BLK needs IDEF and REB. Otherwise, the player may create highlights without consistently ending possessions.

### Drafting CLU before the identity exists

CLU should close a build, not define an empty one. Secure the position’s daily responsibilities first.

### Ignoring mode risk

A specialist that works in Classic may fail in Chaos. When position is hidden, choose Build a Hooper attributes that remain useful across multiple roles.

## A practical 13-round attribute plan

Use this simple sequence when the board is unpredictable:

1. **Rounds 1–4:** establish the primary role with two or three essential Build a Hooper attributes.
2. **Rounds 5–8:** complete the primary cluster and add one secondary skill.
3. **Rounds 9–11:** repair the most dangerous positional weakness.
4. **Rounds 12–13:** add CLU, ATH, STR, or another supporting rating without breaking the identity.

After the reveal, judge the sheet by role clarity, not only overall rating. Ask whether the player can create a reliable shot, perform the position’s defensive job, and survive an 82-game schedule.

## Build a Hooper attributes FAQ

### What is the most important attribute?

There is no universal answer. HAN and PAS are essential for a floor-general PG, while REB and IDEF are essential for a traditional center. The best Build a Hooper attributes are the ones required by the role you selected.

### Should I always take an A+ grade?

No. Take the A+ when it supports the build. Skip it when a lower grade fills a position-critical hole.

### Is 3PT better than MID?

3PT usually creates more spacing, but MID can become a strong second scoring level or a fallback when the draft does not offer outside shooting. A balanced scorer can use both.

### Is DNK the same as FIN?

No. FIN covers general rim conversion and touch. DNK covers above-the-rim finishing. FIN is usually more broadly useful.

### Which attributes are safest in Chaos?

FIN, ATH, HAN, PDEF, REB, and IDEF tend to travel across multiple positions. The best combination depends on the players and team seasons you spin.
