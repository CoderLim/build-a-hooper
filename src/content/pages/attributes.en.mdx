## The 13 Attributes Explained

![Retro tactical poster mapping thirteen basketball attributes around an anonymous player](/imgs/generated/attributes-tactical-poster.webp)

Every Build a Hooper sheet fills the same thirteen grades. Modes change what you see. Positions change which grades you should prioritize. The attributes themselves stay fixed:

**3PT · MID · FIN · DNK · HAN · PAS · PDEF · IDEF · BLK · REB · ATH · STR · CLU**

This page is the encyclopedia. For first-run sequencing, use [/how-to-play](/how-to-play). For position priorities, use [/best-builds](/best-builds).

Letter grades in the game are a fan simulation mapped from a reference rating set — illustrative, not official NBA or 2K data.

## How to read the sheet

Think in **clusters**, not thirteen disconnected upgrades:

1. **Scoring** — 3PT, MID, FIN, DNK
2. **Creation** — HAN, PAS
3. **Defense / glass** — PDEF, IDEF, BLK, REB
4. **Physical / close** — ATH, STR, CLU

Strong cards reinforce one cluster and borrow from a second. Empty creation with elite shooting dies when the first option is taken away. Elite paint defense with no finishing stalls on offense. That is basketball, not only a video-game rule — roster construction research keeps rediscovering the same need for complementary roles ([Frontiers on archetype mix](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2025.1639431/full)).

After three locks, name the role out loud. If you cannot, you are collecting grades.

## Scoring cluster

### 3PT — Three-point shooting

**On the floor:** Spacing. Forces help defenders to respect the arc so drives and post touches have air.

**In the draft:** Priority for SG and stretch bigs; high value for modern PG/SF. A low 3PT on a guard who also lacks MID/FIN leaves no scoring axis.

**Draft tip:** Do not stack only corner-shooter profiles if HAN is empty — movement and pull-up creation need handles too ([Basketball Index movement / off-screen shooter roles](https://www.bball-index.com/offensive-archetypes/)).

### MID — Mid-range

**On the floor:** Pull-ups, elbows, short fadeaways. Less fashionable than threes in real analytics debates, still useful when the arc is covered or you need a two in the half-court.

**In the draft:** Pairs with 3PT for three-level scorers; pairs with FIN for mid-post forwards. Pure MID without spacing or paint threat is easier to scheme against across 82 games.

**Draft tip:** If 3PT keeps missing on the board, MID + FIN can still feed an SG/SF scorer — especially in Blind when you cannot grade-shop the arc.

### FIN — Finishing

**On the floor:** Layups, floaters, touch around the rim through contact. Not the same as dunking highlight reels.

**In the draft:** Near-universal value. Guards who attack closeouts, wings who cut, bigs who roll all need it. Empty FIN with high DNK looks athletic and still bricks easies.

**Draft tip:** Take FIN earlier than pride allows. Seasons expose finishers who only dunk in space.

### DNK — Dunking / above-rim finishing

**On the floor:** Vertical threat, lob finishes, putback authority. Flashy, situational.

**In the draft:** Strong complement to ATH and FIN for slashers and rim runners. Weak as a lone scoring skill — you still need a way to get there (HAN/ATH) or a roll man context (screens, PAS from teammates you do not control the same way in this sim).

**Draft tip:** DNK is a spice attribute. Do not let it steal a lock that should have been REB or PAS for your role.

## Creation cluster

### HAN — Handles / ball security

**On the floor:** Live dribble, escape pressure, start offense without turning it over.

**In the draft:** Non-negotiable for primary ball handlers. High value for any shot creator. Lower priority for pure roll bigs — but Chaos drafts should keep some HAN because position might land on a guard.

**Draft tip:** PG runs that skip HAN to chase 3PT often collapse when the season gets physical. Creation without the ball is not creation.

### PAS — Passing / playmaking

**On the floor:** Assist creation, skip passes, pocket feeds, decision speed.

**In the draft:** Floor generals live here. Point forwards and playmaking bigs (think hub profiles in [NetStats-style role talk](https://netstats.dev/archetypes)) also lean PAS. Scoring guards can survive with secondary PAS; empty PAS on a high-usage PG is a long-season problem.

**Draft tip:** PAS without HAN still helps off the catch. HAN without PAS creates iso-only cards. For PG, aim to secure both before round 9 when you can.

## Defense / glass cluster

### PDEF — Perimeter defense

**On the floor:** Contain ball handlers, fight over screens, contest catch-and-shoot looks. Wings who switch 1–3 live here; scouting notes for modern wings treat switchability as a draft separator ([HoopBrief wing traits](https://hoopbrief.com/blog/what-nba-scouts-look-for-in-wings)).

**In the draft:** Priority for SG/SF two-way builds; useful for smaller PGs. Bigs can leave it lower if IDEF/BLK/REB are elite — but Blind/Chaos hybrids should not zero it out.

### IDEF — Interior defense

**On the floor:** Paint presence, post containment, deterring drives without needing a block every time.

**In the draft:** Core for PF/C anchors. Stretch fours who abandon IDEF become liabilities when the opponent parks size inside. Guards rarely primary-stack IDEF unless building a novelty lockdown card.

### BLK — Shot blocking

**On the floor:** Rim protection events and help rotations from behind.

**In the draft:** Complements IDEF for true anchors. High BLK with empty REB still loses long possessions. Treat BLK as the highlight sibling of interior defense, not a full paint kit.

### REB — Rebounding

**On the floor:** End possessions, create second chances, punish small lineups.

**In the draft:** Mandatory for C, near-mandatory for PF. Wings who crash can use moderate REB. Guards usually take crumbs last — unless Chaos forces a big reveal and you underbuilt the glass.

## Physical / close cluster

### ATH — Athleticism

**On the floor:** First step, recovery speed, transition burst, functional bounce.

**In the draft:** Multiplier for slashers, pressure defenders, and rim runners. Low ATH with high FIN still works for crafty finishers; low ATH with high PDEF struggles to stay in front.

### STR — Strength

**On the floor:** Hold position, finish through contact, post leverage, screen durability.

**In the draft:** Bigs and physical wings love it. Pure spacers can live with less. Empty STR on a “paint bully” identity is a contradiction the season will notice.

### CLU — Clutch / closing execution

**On the floor:** Late-game shot-making and composure when possessions shrink.

**In the draft:** Valuable closer for every position. Bad trap: spending early locks on CLU while the primary cluster is empty. Clutch does not invent a shot diet you never built.

**Draft tip:** Target CLU in the back half once the role is real — unless a once-per-run A+ CLU appears on an otherwise perfect donor in Classic.

## What matters most?

There is no universal ranking. Position and role decide:

| If you are building… | Lean early on…                          |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Floor general PG     | HAN, PAS, then 3PT or MID               |
| Scoring SG           | 3PT or MID + FIN, then HAN              |
| 3-and-D wing         | 3PT + PDEF, then ATH/REB                |
| Paint PF/C           | REB + IDEF, then FIN/STR/BLK            |
| Chaos unknown        | FIN or REB + one creation + one defense |

Across roles, empty **creation** or empty **finishing** are the two holes that most often ruin “pretty” overall ratings by game 60.
