## What makes a Build a Hooper build good?

A strong player answers four questions.

### 1. What is the player’s primary job?

The build should be easy to describe. “Floor general,” “movement shooter,” “3-and-D wing,” “stretch four,” and “paint anchor” are useful descriptions because each one points to a connected group of attributes. “A lot of A grades” is not a role.

### 2. Does the position’s basic responsibility exist?

Position fit matters more than a luxury skill. A PG without HAN or PAS has no reliable engine. A C without REB cannot finish possessions. A stretch PF still needs enough REB and IDEF to function as a big. The best Build a Hooper builds protect the nightly job before chasing highlights.

### 3. Is there a second way to contribute?

A primary strength can be stopped. Shooters need FIN, HAN, or defense. Paint scorers need REB and IDEF. Passers need a scoring threat. Defenders need enough offense to stay on the floor. A secondary cluster turns a narrow specialist into a season-ready player.

### 4. Are the weaknesses acceptable?

No build will be perfect. The objective is to leave weaknesses in low-priority areas, not in the position’s foundation. A traditional center can accept weak PDEF more easily than weak REB. A movement shooter can accept weak BLK more easily than weak 3PT or HAN.

## Best point guard builds

Point guard is the creation position. The best Build a Hooper point guard builds begin with **HAN** and **PAS**, then add one scoring axis through 3PT, MID, or FIN.

### Floor General

- **Primary:** HAN, PAS
- **Support:** 3PT or MID, CLU, PDEF
- **Best for:** consistent offense and controlled seasons

The Floor General is the safest PG template because it performs the position’s basic job every possession. Add enough shooting that defenders cannot ignore the ball handler.

### Shot Creator

- **Primary:** HAN, 3PT or MID, FIN
- **Support:** PAS, ATH, CLU
- **Best for:** high-usage scoring guards

The Shot Creator needs secondary PAS. Without it, the player becomes an isolation scorer who cannot punish help defense.

### Two-Way Lead Guard

- **Primary:** HAN, PDEF, PAS or 3PT
- **Support:** ATH, FIN, CLU
- **Best for:** balanced guard play

This route is harder because creation and defense compete for early picks, but it can produce one of the most stable Build a Hooper builds over a full season.

## Best shooting guard builds

Shooting guard should create points without becoming useless when the first shot is removed. The best Build a Hooper shooting guard builds establish a scoring axis, then add FIN, HAN, or PDEF.

### Movement Shooter

- **Primary:** 3PT, ATH, enough HAN
- **Support:** MID, CLU, PDEF
- **Best for:** spacing and off-ball scoring

### Three-Level Scorer

- **Primary:** 3PT, MID, FIN
- **Support:** HAN, ATH, CLU
- **Best for:** adaptable half-court scoring

### Two-Way Scorer

- **Primary:** 3PT, PDEF, FIN or HAN
- **Support:** ATH, CLU
- **Best for:** stable playoff value

A shooting guard with only DNK or only 3PT is easier to scheme against. The best Build a Hooper builds at SG create at least two credible scoring responses.

## Best small forward builds

Small forward is the most flexible position. It can become a shooter, defender, slasher, rebounder, or secondary creator. That versatility is useful, but it also tempts players to spread picks across too many unrelated ratings.

### 3-and-D Wing

- **Primary:** 3PT, PDEF, ATH
- **Support:** FIN, REB, CLU
- **Best for:** reliable two-way impact

### Slashing Forward

- **Primary:** FIN, ATH, DNK or STR
- **Support:** 3PT, HAN, CLU
- **Best for:** transition and rim pressure

### Point Forward

- **Primary:** HAN, PAS, 3PT
- **Support:** PDEF, FIN, CLU
- **Best for:** secondary creation from the wing

The 3-and-D Wing is one of the safest Build a Hooper builds because shooting and perimeter defense remain useful in nearly every matchup. The Point Forward has a higher creation ceiling but requires both HAN and PAS to justify the label.

## Best power forward builds

Power forward sits between wing skill and center responsibility. REB and IDEF usually form the base. The offensive route determines whether the player becomes an enforcer, spacer, or rim runner.

### Interior Enforcer

- **Primary:** REB, IDEF, FIN, STR
- **Support:** BLK, CLU, MID
- **Best for:** physical two-way paint play

### Stretch Four

- **Primary:** 3PT, REB, IDEF
- **Support:** MID, FIN, CLU
- **Best for:** spacing without losing big-man responsibilities

### Rim Runner

- **Primary:** FIN, ATH, DNK, REB
- **Support:** IDEF, STR, CLU
- **Best for:** vertical finishing and transition

The most common PF mistake is treating “stretch” as permission to ignore REB and IDEF. The best Build a Hooper builds at power forward add spacing after establishing enough size-related value to survive inside.

## Best center builds

Center has the clearest foundation: **REB + IDEF**. From there, the player can add BLK for a paint anchor, FIN and STR for a power finisher, or PAS and 3PT for a specialty playmaking big.

### Paint Anchor

- **Primary:** REB, IDEF, BLK, STR
- **Support:** FIN, CLU
- **Best for:** defense, rebounding, and stable team results

### Power Finisher

- **Primary:** FIN, REB, STR, DNK
- **Support:** IDEF, ATH, CLU
- **Best for:** rim scoring and offensive rebounding

### Playmaking Stretch Five

- **Primary:** REB, PAS, 3PT or MID
- **Support:** IDEF, HAN, CLU
- **Best for:** advanced players attempting a rare hub-big route

The Playmaking Stretch Five is the most difficult center template because it asks the draft to provide specialty skills without sacrificing the foundation. New players should master the Paint Anchor first.

## Build a Hooper best builds by draft phase

### Rounds 1–4: secure the foundation

Use early picks on the ratings that make the position functional. PG wants HAN/PAS, SG wants a scoring axis, SF wants a wing identity, PF wants REB/IDEF, and C wants REB/IDEF. Avoid CLU, DNK, or specialty ratings unless the primary job is already developing.

### Rounds 5–9: complete the archetype

This phase separates a role from a collection of grades. Finish the primary cluster and choose the secondary contribution. A 3-and-D wing adds ATH or FIN. A Paint Anchor adds BLK or STR. A Shot Creator adds PAS before the build becomes isolation-only.

### Rounds 10–13: patch weaknesses

The last rounds should protect the season. Add missing PDEF, FIN, REB, ATH, STR, or CLU depending on the position. Do not create a new archetype with three picks remaining.

## How modes change the best builds

The same role behaves differently across the three [Build a Hooper modes](/modes).

### Classic

Classic is the best environment for precise Build a Hooper builds. The position and grades are visible, and three rerolls allow you to target specific rating combinations. Follow the detailed position priorities closely.

### Blind

Blind reveals the position but hides ratings. Keep the same positional responsibilities, but choose donors by known basketball role rather than visible letters. Build a backup route in case the hidden grades do not support the ideal archetype.

### Chaos

Chaos hides position and ratings. The best Build a Hooper builds in Chaos use portable combinations: FIN + ATH, HAN + PDEF, 3PT + FIN, or REB + IDEF. A hyper-specialized PG or C template becomes risky when the final position can change.

## How to choose the right build

Choose a build based on three factors:

1. **Your position knowledge:** use a role you understand well enough to identify its essential attributes.
2. **The current board:** do not force a shooting route through thirteen bad shooting spins when the draft is offering an elite defensive or interior path.
3. **The mode:** Classic supports specialization, Blind supports position-based flexibility, and Chaos rewards portability.

A good pivot does not abandon all previous picks. It finds a neighboring role that uses the same foundation. A PG Floor General can become a scoring lead guard if shooting appears and PAS stalls. A Stretch Four can become an Interior Enforcer if 3PT disappears but REB, IDEF, FIN, and STR develop.

## Common best-build mistakes

### Copying a list without reading the board

Priority lists are direction, not a script. Use the best available compatible rating rather than waiting forever for one exact sequence.

### Maxing a highlight attribute too early

DNK, BLK, and CLU are useful, but they rarely replace creation, a scoring axis, rebounding, or interior defense.

### Ignoring a second scoring response

A pure shooter needs FIN or HAN. A pure rim scorer needs spacing, creation, or physical support. A single attack type creates volatile seasons.

### Calling a build two-way without defense

A scorer becomes two-way only when PDEF, IDEF, BLK, REB, ATH, or STR creates a real defensive job.

### Using overall rating as the only test

Overall rating cannot tell you whether the build fits its position. Read the [attribute sheet](/attributes), role, record, and playoff result together.

## Build a Hooper best builds FAQ

### What is the easiest build for beginners?

A Floor General PG, 3-and-D SF, or Paint Anchor C has a clear priority list and teaches how connected attributes work.

### What build gets the highest overall rating?

There is no guaranteed route. Overall depends on the grades collected, while season success also depends on role coherence and position fit.

### What is the safest Chaos build?

A balanced player with FIN, ATH, one creation skill, and one defensive or rebounding skill usually survives more position outcomes than a narrow specialist.

### Should every build have 3PT?

No. 3PT is valuable, but an Interior Enforcer or Paint Anchor can succeed through REB, IDEF, FIN, STR, and BLK. The build still needs a reliable offensive contribution.

### When should I take CLU?

Usually after the primary role exists. CLU is strongest as a late or middle-round finishing tool, not as the first foundation pick.

### Can I change the build mid-draft?

Yes. Pivot when the new route uses several existing ratings and the board repeatedly supports it. Do not pivot into a completely unrelated role late in the draft.
