# Best Builds by Position

![Retro half-court tactical poster showing five complementary basketball positions](/imgs/generated/best-builds-tactical-poster.webp)

These guides translate real basketball **jobs** into Build a Hooper’s thirteen-attribute sheet. They are not a promise of a perfect overall or an undefeated season. They are role templates: what to lock early, what to patch late, and which modern archetypes map cleanly onto PG / SG / SF / PF / C.

Grounding comes from public role writing — floor generals and shot creators, 3-and-D wings, stretch bigs, rim anchors — not from copying another fan site’s weight table ([Basketball Index roles](https://www.bball-index.com/offensive-archetypes/), [position skill primers](https://halloffame-online.com/blog/basketball-positions-explained-roles-responsibilities-skills/), [wing scouting traits](https://hoopbrief.com/blog/what-nba-scouts-look-for-in-wings)).

## The consistency principle

Across 82 games, a coherent B+/A- sheet beats a chaotic A+ highlight reel with empty handles or empty glass. Real roster research keeps finding the same pattern: teams need complementary roles, not thirteen maxed box-score lines ([archetype mix research](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2025.1639431/full)).

In Build a Hooper terms:

1. Name the role by lock three.
2. Feed its primary cluster.
3. Patch one weakness that your position faces every night.
4. Let CLU and spice attributes come late.

## How modes change these builds

- **Classic** — follow the deep guide literally; you can see grades.
- **Blind** — keep the same priorities, but select donors by known role when grades are hidden.
- **Chaos** — bias toward attributes that survive multiple position reveals (FIN, REB, HAN, PDEF). Hyper-specialized snipers break more often.
